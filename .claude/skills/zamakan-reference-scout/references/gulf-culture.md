# Gulf calendar and cultural-fit guide

Use it to search the right moments and to score `cultural_fit`. Hijri dates depend on moon sighting:
treat them as **approximate (±1–2 days)** and confirm closer to the date.

## Cultural-fit checklist (score 0–5)
Start at 5 and subtract for each issue. Note the reason in `cultural_fit.notes`.
- **Worship is never trivialised.** Prayer, the adhan, Quran recitation and the Kaaba are not props, punchlines
  or brand sounds. The social side of Ramadan (iftar, suhoor, gatherings, generosity) is fair ground.
- **Daytime food and drink during Ramadan.** Avoid showing eating or drinking in daylight in Ramadan creative;
  set consumption after iftar or at suhoor.
- **Modesty and representation.** Women appear widely in Saudi advertising now; keep styling modest and
  respectful, and check the client's own stance. Avoid romantic framing.
- **Dialect and casting.** A Saudi audience notices a pan-Arab or Egyptian voice on a Saudi brand. Prefer
  Saudi dialect (white dialect for broad reach) and Saudi talent for KSA campaigns.
- **Clichés lower freshness and fit:** generic lanterns and crescents, camels at sunset, falcons as filler,
  oud stings, endless desert drone shots, "Ramadan Kareem" greeting films with no idea.
- **Regulation.** No alcohol, pork, gambling or explicit content; follow GCAM (General Commission for Audiovisual Media) rules for
  ads and influencer disclosure; health and finance claims need substantiation. Flag, don't guess.
- **Music.** Some audiences and brands avoid music-heavy creative in Ramadan, so note it as a risk rather than a ban.
- **National symbols.** The flag carries the Shahada: never print it on disposable items or alter it. Treat the
  emblem, the King and the Crown Prince with care, and don't put them in commercial creative.

## What "good fit" looks like
- A Saudi behaviour shown with affection (hospitality rivalry, late-night gatherings, Najdi generosity)
- Heritage that feels present and personal, not museum-like
- Humour that laughs *with* people, not at a region or dialect
